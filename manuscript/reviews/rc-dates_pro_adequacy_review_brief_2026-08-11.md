# Cover note for the second-round independent adequacy review

## Purpose

Please conduct a fresh, adversarial but measured review of the revised manuscript and its supporting
evidence as a dress rehearsal for journal submission after a major presubmission review. Assume no
prior knowledge of the project. The central question is no longer whether the first-round report
could motivate a larger research programme; it is whether the present bounded paper is
scientifically defensible, clearly framed and ready for submission to the *Journal of Archaeological
Science*.

The first external report recommended major revision. The authors accepted its central concerns but
chose a deliberately lean repair: resolve the inferential defects that could change the paper's
conclusions, narrow the estimand and claims to what the design actually supports, and state residual
scope openly. Please judge the adequacy of that response rather than rewarding additional analysis
for its own sake.

## Suggested reading order

1. Read `01_REVISED_SUBMISSION/anonymous_manuscript.pdf`, the supplementary information and the
   five main figures as a fresh reviewer, without first reading the response documents.
2. Read `02_REVIEW_HISTORY/first_external_review_2026-08-09.md` and
   `stage4_response_crosswalk.md`.
3. Audit consequential claims against `03_REVIEW_REPOSITORY/`, especially the prespecified designs,
   accepted summaries, validation records, checksums, active code and focused tests.
4. Inspect the cover letter, highlights, title page and submission checklist for consistency and
   journal readiness. The title page and cover letter are author-facing submission files; the
   manuscript and nested review repository are author-anonymized.

## What changed after the first review

1. **The construct was narrowed.** The paper now studies post hoc within-site retention among
   exchangeable dates. It no longer presents the simulation as prospective context selection,
   occupation-history sampling, onset/termination inference or a universal field-sampling rule.
2. **Directional model space was repaired.** Five generating truths—uniform, linear increase,
   linear decrease, exponential increase and exponential decrease—are crossed with three treated-site
   proportions, eight retention rules and 1,000 paired replicates per scenario. Fitted linear and
   exponential models can have either sign.
3. **The empirical comparison was rebuilt.** SPD, frequency-distribution and KDE curves now use the
   same 540 terrestrial charcoal/wood dates from 68 sites, common calendar bins and unit-integral
   normalization. Shell and human-bone dates are excluded rather than assigned unmodelled reservoir
   corrections. Selection uses calibrated posterior medians with deterministic tie-breaking.
4. **Visual impressions were replaced by quantitative gates.** Total-variation distances separate
   baseline method disagreement from the deformation induced by retention. Nine prespecified
   empirical sensitivity configurations test the oldest- and youngest-retention conclusions.
5. **The inferential language was corrected.** Ordinary likelihood and AIC claims are reserved for
   the primary `l1` analysis. `l2` is described as an expected-log-density scoring sensitivity.
   Directional state recovery and generating-family recovery are reported separately.
6. **All display items and submission text were reconciled.** Figures 1–5, Supplementary Figure S1,
   captions, abstract, discussion, highlights and cover letter now report the signed and
   common-membership evidence consistently.
7. **Limitations and archive governance were made explicit.** The manuscript distinguishes code,
   synthetic/frozen outputs and aggregate empirical evidence from source archaeological records that
   remain subject to licensing, sensitive-location review and Indigenous data-governance decisions.

## Deliberately bounded residual scope

The revision did **not** add a hierarchical site-history generator, weaker or pulsed truths, a
matched-total-date allocation experiment, or a new realised-noise experiment covering the one-sided
oldest and youngest rules. The original realised-noise v1 decision remains labelled **FAIL** (15 of
18 checks passed; three failed), and v2 remains a separately labelled post-unblinding diagnostic.
The paper's ten-fold generating contrast is stated repeatedly and random multi-date retention is not
promoted as a general field recommendation.

These omissions are not presumed acceptable. Please decide whether the narrowed claims and explicit
limitations make them reasonable boundaries for this paper, or whether any one of them still blocks
submission.

## Questions requiring a decision

Please give direct answers to the following.

1. Is the exchangeable-date retention result sufficiently consequential and archaeologically useful
   for *JAS* without a site-history generator?
2. Has the signed-model repair removed the direction-forcing defect, and do the manuscript's
   direction and family-recovery claims match the evidence?
3. Does the common-membership empirical analysis support the ordering claimed for oldest, youngest
   and bracketed retention? Is bracketed retention overgeneralized because the full nine-way
   sensitivity gate was applied only to oldest and youngest?
4. Is omission of realised observation noise for the headline one-sided rules adequately disclosed,
   or does it require one final analysis before submission?
5. Are the strong ten-fold signal and other simplifying assumptions qualified sufficiently to
   prevent readers from interpreting the results as universal sampling guidance?
6. Are likelihood, AIC, scoring-rule and effect-size terms now technically correct and consistently
   used?
7. Do the abstract, results, figures, discussion, highlights and cover letter make only claims that
   can be traced to the supplied evidence?
8. Is the author-anonymized repository scientifically useful despite excluding raw and record-level
   AustArch data? Are its exclusions and remaining data-governance actions described honestly?
9. Have any major first-round criticisms been answered rhetorically rather than analytically, or
   disappeared from the crosswalk without adequate resolution?
10. What is the smallest additional revision, if any, that would materially change your submission
    recommendation?

## Requested report

Please return:

- a one-paragraph editorial recommendation: **submit**, **minor targeted revision**, **major revision**
  or **hold**;
- an issue-by-issue M1–M10 adequacy matrix, classifying each point as resolved, adequately bounded,
  partly resolved or unresolved;
- a claims-to-evidence audit of the abstract, headline results and conclusion;
- only decision-changing scientific, statistical, archaeological-method or reproducibility concerns;
- concise line edits where wording is materially inaccurate, ambiguous or likely to mislead;
- a final submission-readiness checklist separating mandatory fixes from optional future work.

Please do not recommend a larger factorial programme merely because it would be interesting. Do
recommend additional work where the present evidence cannot support a consequential claim. Apply
the standard expected of a demanding external reviewer while keeping the current paper's tight,
targeted contribution in view.
