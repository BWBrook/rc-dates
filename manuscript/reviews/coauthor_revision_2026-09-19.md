# rc-dates: revision following fresh external review

19 September 2026

The independent Astra review found the central empirical and simulation results
well supported, with a necessary correction to Figure 4 and a smaller disclosure
gap in the historical observation-noise sensitivity. This revision addresses
those findings before the authors' detailed editing pass.

## What changed

Figure 4 now reports a bounded contrast between recent and old endpoint
densities. This remains interpretable when a fitted linear density reaches zero
at one endpoint, where its log density ratio is infinite and a finite numerical
approximation depends on optimiser tolerance. Each fitted replicate is
transformed before computing the mean and central 95% range. Every group shows
its original near-boundary frequency, including the 7.2% and 8.2% frequencies
under random-1 retention of increasing and decreasing linear histories.

The original fits, model selections and scientific decisions are retained. The
new contrast compresses large endpoint ratios; it is neither a growth rate nor
evidence of recovery of the complete density shape. The manuscript and
supplement distinguish the natural linear boundary from the exponential
model's imposed rate cap.

The historical noise sensitivity is now explicitly described as competition
between uniform and growth-only linear/exponential candidates. It therefore
does not validate the current signed model competition. Its original failed
prespecified assessment and subsequent post-unblinding diagnostic remain
separate, and noise effects on the extrema rules remain untested.

The Introduction now distinguishes this post hoc retention experiment from
earlier work on prospective sample selection and unequal site contributions.
Bibliographic details have been corrected and Figures 3 and 5 have larger
labels.

## Review and reproduction files

The full adversarial review is supplied alongside the revised manuscript and
supplement. The scientific archive is
`rc-dates-scientific-reproducibility-2026-09-19.zip`; its validation record states
the checks performed and remaining limits. The 11 September archive is retained
unchanged. No new simulation campaign was needed for this correction.

The paper's scope remains the same: a test of post hoc date retention and an
empirical benchmark, rather than a reconstruction of Sahul population history
or a recommendation for allocating future dating effort. Co-author approval,
authorship/declaration checks and public-release arrangements remain to be
settled before submission.
