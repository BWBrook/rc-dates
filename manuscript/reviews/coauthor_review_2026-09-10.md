# rc-dates: co-author review, 10 September 2026

*Updated 11 September following Fable’s reply; the packet retains its original circulation date.*

## Assessment

The paper is ready for co-author circulation. Its strongest result is that a regional trend can follow the rule used to retain dates within sites, even when the underlying trend runs in the opposite direction. The AustArch benchmark shows that selecting each site's oldest or youngest date can also change empirical temporal shape more than the differences among SPD, FD and KDE summaries. Together these support a useful methodological warning, with a clear boundary: the study tests post hoc retention among exchangeable regional ages, not occupation histories or a reconstruction of Sahul population size.

My recommendation is to keep this focused argument for *Journal of Archaeological Science*. The main editorial risk is that readers regard the extrema effect as expected order-statistic behaviour and question its archaeological relevance. The contribution is the quantified consequence for a radiocarbon-analysis workflow, its comparison with count-matched random retention, and its empirical demonstration. The co-authors' archaeological judgement will be most useful in making that relevance convincing without implying that the tested rules describe how often real databases were curated this way.

This fresh review used GPT-6 Astra, with an independent scientific review by GPT-5.6 Sol and a subsequent outsider text review by Claude Fable 5.1. Fable correctly challenged the initial text-readiness verdict; this version incorporates a further readability pass before circulation. ## Changes in this version

- **Made the result easier to read.** The abstract, highlights and Results now lead with the retention mechanism and the constant-history result. The empirical comparison reports the same 68-date count for oldest, youngest and random-1: SPD mean shifts of +3,133, −2,439 and +103 years, respectively, with a random-1 central 95% range of −433 to +594. Repeated caveats and internal revision labels have been reduced, and the SI separates methodological explanation from archive commands and hashes.
- **Preserved the consequential disagreements.** Bracketed retention's +352-year SPD shift is inside the random-1 shift range, but its SPD total variation exceeds the corresponding random-1 range; random-1 also retains half as many dates. This does not establish equivalence to random subsampling. Nor does the independent-age experiment establish a lower bound on prospective selection bias. The abstract retains a brief observation-model qualification because the headline extrema rules remain untested under realised noise. The response to Fable records these decisions and their evidence. Fable subsequently withdrew the bracketed and lower-bound claims and agreed the text is ready to circulate. Section 4.3 now explains why independent per-date noise preserves the median-selection mechanism while leaving the AIC response untested.

- **Clarified what model selection establishes.** Bracketed retention can produce a U-shaped distribution under uniform truth. A uniform model winning against monotonic alternatives does not establish recovery of that shape. The main text, Figure 3 caption and a short exact-age derivation in Supplementary Section S2.6 now explain this.
- **Recognised the existing count-matched comparisons.** Oldest and youngest match random-1; bracketed matches random-2 in the simulations. The previous limitation incorrectly suggested that all rank effects were confounded with retained count. Comparisons of different random retention counts still do not identify an optimal allocation of a fixed dating budget, and the empirical bracketed experiment lacks random-2.
- **Explained partial-site treatment.** Untreated sites retain all dates; treated sites retain one or two. The percentage of treated sites therefore exceeds their share of retained observations. The partial-treatment findings do not establish a general harm threshold.
- **Tightened statistical language.** All-replicate simulation outcomes are no longer called deterministic. Total variation is defined in terms of bin probability masses. The 99.75% scoring agreement is correctly labelled agreement on both family and direction. Scenario-replicate combinations are distinguished from independent simulated datasets.
- **Prepared the circulation files.** The abstract is more direct, the cover letter is shorter and explicitly a draft, and the title page no longer claims that all authors have already approved the submitted version. AI declarations include the current review and the reported Claude/Opus contribution; the authors should confirm the complete tool/version details.

## Decisions for the co-authors

1. **Archaeological interpretation.** Does the bounded retention mechanism, with the AustArch benchmark, capture the contribution we want to make? Concrete examples of relevant compilation practices would strengthen the framing if the authors can substantiate them. The current text does not claim a measured prevalence of those practices.
2. **Final analytical scope.** The most valuable additional test, if required before submission, is realised observation noise for oldest and youngest retention. The earlier sensitivity did not test these headline rules. Its prespecified v1 result remains FAIL (15/18 gates); the later v2 diagnostic remains explicitly post-unblinding. My recommendation is to circulate now and resolve this scope question with the co-authors before commissioning more computation.
3. **Authorship and release details.** Confirm author order, affiliations, funding, CRediT contributions and AI-use details. Before journal submission, record approval of the final version and declarations, and settle the repository/deposit and permitted aggregate-data release arrangements.
