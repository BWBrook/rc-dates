# rc-dates: October revision

19 October 2026

The paper now centres on a practical distinction: giving sites equal numbers of
dates does not ensure that their retained ages represent the regional history.
Its title is **Choosing among a site's radiocarbon dates can create or reverse
regional trends**. The opening uses the empirical comparison to make the problem
concrete, then the simulations establish the mechanism and its consequences.

## What changed

The Introduction and Discussion have been rebuilt for both a general-area editor
and a specialist reader. A new analytic Figure 1 explains why selecting a site's
oldest or youngest date changes the age distribution. The simulation results now
precede the empirical results. The all-date empirical figure has moved to the
supplement, leaving six main figures and three supplementary figures.

The new experiment tests weaker histories, with end-to-end ratios of 1.5, 2, 3
and 5. It adds 48 scenarios with 500 replicates each to the accepted constant and
ten-fold experiments. At 75% site treatment, the opposing one-sided rule reversed
direction in 93–100% of replicates for 1.5- and two-fold histories, compared with
none for ten-fold histories. At full treatment it reversed every replicate at
every tested ratio. These results replace the earlier limitation that only
strong changes had been tested.

Avoiding reversal is a weaker result than recovering direction. For example,
full bracketed retention recovered the direction of 1.5-fold histories in only
10.6–16.8% of replicates, usually selecting uniform instead. Count-matched
random-2 retention recovered direction in 60.6–66.6%. The manuscript separates
these outcomes from recovery of the generating family and makes no claim for a
universally safe retention proportion or minimum number of dates.

## Evidence and remaining scope

An independent aggregation of 1,872,000 stored fitted rows reproduced all 1,008
selection-rate summaries exactly. The original 20-replicate bridge check also
matched all 960 rows and 17 result columns. The numerical registry now contains
111 checked values. The scientific archive includes the prespecified design,
the disclosed display amendment, original display and logs, accepted synthetic
outputs, and commands to reconstruct summaries and rerun simulations. Its
`VALIDATION.md` records the executable checks and their limits.

The original failed observation-noise assessment and the later post-unblinding
diagnostic remain separate. Realised observation noise has still not been
tested for oldest, youngest or bracketed retention. The empirical analysis is a
conditional benchmark, not a reconstruction of Sahul population history.

This packet is for co-author review. The named scientific ZIP excludes source
and record-level archaeological data. Author details, declarations, final
approval, review anonymity and public-release arrangements remain to be settled
before submission. Earlier review packets are retained.
