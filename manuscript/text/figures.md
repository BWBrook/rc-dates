![Baseline aggregate smoothed curves for the AustArch reference dataset. Plotted are the summed probability distribution (SPD; sum of calibrated posteriors followed by a 100-year running mean), 100-year binned frequency distribution (FD; displayed with a two-bin running mean), and Gaussian kernel density estimate (KDE; bandwidth = 100 yr) centred on each date's weighted-mean calibrated age. Each curve is normalised to unit area; for joint display the SPD and KDE were re-binned to the FD 100-year grid. This is the full-dataset baseline against which Figure 2's sub-sampling scenarios are compared. The display uses a padded 12,000-0 cal BP plotting range, while the analysis window is 11,650-651 cal BP. Displays here are descriptive only; inferential results use likelihoods built directly from calibrated posteriors. Axes: years cal BP and scaled density ($\times 10^{-4}$).](../figures/fig_1.png){#fig-main-1 width=95%}

![Consequences of within-site sub-sampling in the AustArch reference set. Rows show oldest, youngest, bracketed (oldest + youngest), random-1, and random-5 rules. Left panels apply each rule to 50% of sites, with untreated sites contributing all dates; right panels apply it to all sites. Curves are unit-area SPD, KDE, and FD summaries, with SPD/KDE re-binned to the 100-year FD grid. Shading gives the central 95% across repeated realisations; full-site oldest, youngest, and bracketed rules are deterministic. The all-dates baseline is in Figure 1. These descriptive panels show how sampling rules reshape aggregate curves; inference uses likelihoods fitted to calibrated posteriors. Axes: padded 12,000-0 cal BP plotting range and scaled value.](../figures/fig_2.png){#fig-main-2 width=58%}

![Accuracy of within-site sampling strategies versus the fraction of sites subjected to the rule. Accuracy is the share of simulation replicates in which AIC selects the generating growth family (uniform, linear, or exponential); the dotted line marks chance accuracy (1/3). Each point pools 1,000 replicates from each family ($n = 3{,}000$); vertical bars are paired cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals from 1,000 resamples of replicate identifiers, retaining the three generating-family outcomes together. The all-dates reference has no sub-sampling intensity and is repeated across the x-axis for comparison. Results are shown for the $\ell^1$ likelihood; the $\ell^2$ variant yields near-identical patterns (Supplementary Information). Axes: percentage of sites sub-sampled (50-100%) and accuracy.](../figures/fig_3.pdf){#fig-main-3 width=95%}

![Parameter bias induced by within-site sampling rules under exponential (left) and linear (right) growth models. Points show mean bias in the recovered parameter; horizontal bars span the 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles of replicate-level bias at each sampling intensity (50%, 75%, 100%). Scales differ between panels to reveal structure; the exponential-rate panel is on the order of $10^{-4}$ and the linear-slope panel on the order of $10^{-8}$. Zero bias is indicated by the dashed line. Youngest-date sampling preferentially selects later contexts and therefore produces the sign-opposed positive exponential-rate bias visible in the left panel. Results are shown for the $\ell^1$ likelihood; the $\ell^2$ variant yields the same qualitative pattern (Supplementary Information).](../figures/fig_4.pdf){#fig-main-4 width=95%}

![Unambiguous support for the generating growth family by sampling strategy and site-subsampling intensity. A replicate counts as successful only when the generating family is the sole model retained. Each point pools 1,000 replicates from each generating family ($n = 3{,}000$); vertical bars are paired cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals from 1,000 resamples of replicate identifiers, retaining the three generating-family outcomes together. The all-dates reference has no sub-sampling intensity and is repeated across the x-axis. Results are shown for the $\ell^1$ likelihood; the $\ell^2$ variant yields the same qualitative ranking (Supplementary Information).
