![Baseline aggregate smoothed curves for the AustArch reference dataset. Plotted are the summed probability distribution (SPD; sum of calibrated posteriors followed by a 100-year running mean), 100-year binned frequency distribution (FD), and Gaussian kernel density estimate (KDE; bandwidth = 100 yr) centred on each date's weighted-mean calibrated age. Each curve is normalised to unit area; for joint display the SPD and KDE were re-binned to the FD 100-year grid. This is the full-dataset baseline against which Figure 2's sub-sampling scenarios are compared. The display uses a padded 12,000-0 cal BP plotting range, while the analysis window is 11,650-651 cal BP. Displays here are descriptive only; inferential results use likelihoods built directly from calibrated posteriors. Axes: years cal BP and scaled density (×10⁻⁴).](../figures/fig_1.png){#fig-main-1 width=95%}

![Consequences of within-site sub-sampling on the AustArch reference dataset. Paired panels show five rules: oldest, youngest, bracketed (oldest + youngest), random-1, and random-5. Each rule is shown with mean ± 95% Monte Carlo ribbon at two sampling intensities: 50% of sites treated by the rule, with the remainder contributing all dates, and 100% treated. Curves plot SPD, KDE, and FD summaries normalised to unit area; SPD/KDE were re-binned to the FD 100-year grid for joint display. The 0% all-dates baseline is shown in Figure 1. These displays show how common field constraints reshape regional aggregates independently of smoother choice; inferential results use posterior-based likelihoods rather than the smoothed curves themselves. Axes: padded 12,000-0 cal BP plotting range and scaled value.](../figures/fig_2.png){#fig-main-2 width=95%}

![Accuracy of within-site sampling strategies versus the fraction of sites subjected to the rule. Accuracy is the share of simulation replicates in which AIC selects the true (generating) growth family (uniform, linear, or exponential); chance accuracy among the three families is 1/3. Points are means; vertical bars are 95% bootstrap intervals (1,000 resamples). The all-dates reference has no sub-sampling intensity and is repeated across the x-axis for comparison. Results are shown for the $\ell^1$ likelihood; the $\ell^2$ variant yields near-identical patterns (Supplementary Information). Axes: percentage of sites sub-sampled (50-100%) and accuracy.](../figures/fig_3.pdf){#fig-main-3 width=95%}

![Parameter bias induced by within-site sampling rules under exponential (left) and linear (right) growth models. Points show mean bias in the recovered parameter with 95% bootstrap intervals, across three sampling intensities of the rule (50%, 75%, 100%). Scales differ between panels to reveal structure; the exponential-rate panel is on the order of 10⁻⁴ and the linear-slope panel on the order of 10⁻⁸. Zero bias is indicated by the dashed line. Youngest-date sampling preferentially selects later contexts and therefore produces the sign-opposed positive exponential-rate bias visible in the left panel. Results are shown for the $\ell^1$ likelihood; the $\ell^2$ variant yields the same qualitative pattern (Supplementary Information).](../figures/fig_4.pdf){#fig-main-4 width=95%}
