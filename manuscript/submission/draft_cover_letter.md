I am pleased to submit our manuscript, *Within-site sampling influences radiocarbon “dates-as-data” demographic reconstructions*, for consideration as an Original Research Article in the *Journal of Archaeological Science*.

Radiocarbon “dates-as-data” approaches are widely used to reconstruct past human demography. Our manuscript tests an earlier and less quantified source of bias: which contexts within each site are selected for dating, and how many determinations each site contributes. A regional dataset can remain biased as it grows if many sites contribute only their oldest, youngest or single dated context.

Using the AustArch database for Sahul, we measure how alternative within-site rules affect SPDs, KDEs and frequency distributions. Conditional simulations with empirically parameterised site structure and known population trajectories quantify model recovery, parameter bias and support. Models are fitted directly to calibrated posteriors, retaining chronological uncertainty without treating an aggregate curve as population history.

Within-site sampling changes the normalised empirical displays more than the choice among SPD, KDE and FD. In the conditional simulations, all dates and random samples of three or five dates retain high recovery, whereas oldest, youngest and random-1 weaken recovery or support and induce directional bias. A paired realised-assay sensitivity preserved this ranking with cellwise shifts below two percentage points; the manuscript distinguishes its original screen from a separately labelled post-unblinding diagnostic.

The manuscript motivates recording per-site date counts, stratigraphic span and selection criteria, and testing plausible selection rules. It states the limits of its idealised simulations rather than proposing a universal dating minimum. We consider this combination of empirical analysis, formal modelling and simulation well matched to *Journal of Archaeological Science*.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, has been approved by all authors, and has no conflicts of interest.
