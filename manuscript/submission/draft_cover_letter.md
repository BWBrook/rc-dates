Dear Professors Martinón-Torres and Nikita,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript, *Within-site date-retention rules can distort radiocarbon “dates-as-data” inference*, for consideration as a Research Article in the *Journal of Archaeological Science*.

Radiocarbon dates-as-data methods inherit a consequential but rarely quantified decision: which completed determinations each archaeological site contributes to a regional dataset. Our study isolates this dataset-formation mechanism without treating post hoc retention as prospective field sampling or its extrema as site histories.

In a common-membership benchmark of 540 terrestrial charcoal or wood determinations from 68 well-replicated AustArch sites, full-site oldest, youngest and bracketed retention changed temporal shape more than the remaining differences among SPD, FD and KDE summaries. We then fitted signed regional event-age-density models directly to calibrated posteriors from controlled simulations. When every site was treated, oldest retention selected a decreasing direction and youngest retention an increasing direction under every generating truth: the adverse rule reversed all non-uniform replicates, and either rule manufactured a trend under uniform truth. Random retention preserved the direction of the strong monotonic truths, although sparse retention weakened linear-versus-exponential family recovery.

The manuscript is deliberately bounded. The simulations use exchangeable regional ages, a strong ten-fold signal and a calibration-curve-mean observation model; they do not include occupation histories, stratigraphy or prospective context selection. An earlier realised-observation-noise screen remains transparently failed under its original prespecified gate and did not test the one-sided rules. We therefore recommend auditable site contributions and sensitivity analysis, not a universal minimum number of dates or a field protocol.

We believe this combination of archaeological database analysis, formal radiocarbon modelling and a clearly identified inferential failure mode is well matched to the methodological scope of the *Journal of Archaeological Science*.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, has been approved by all authors and has no conflicts of interest. Data inclusion remains subject to licensing, sensitive-location review and Indigenous data-governance requirements.
